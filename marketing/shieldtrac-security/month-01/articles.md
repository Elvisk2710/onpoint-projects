## Week 1: Seven questions to ask a security company

**Suggested slug:** `choosing-a-security-company`

A security service should fit the property, its use and the client's priorities. Before comparing prices, ask how the service will operate and what responsibilities will be agreed in writing.

## Ask about the people
First, how are guards selected and vetted? Second, what training is relevant to the proposed assignment? Third, how are guards supervised? Ask for clear explanations and evidence where appropriate rather than relying on broad claims.

## Ask about communication
Fourth, what reports will you receive? Fifth, how are incidents escalated? Agree the contact people and how instructions will be updated. Avoid sharing operational details publicly while making these arrangements.

## Ask about systems and scope
Sixth, how will equipment, maintenance and system faults be handled? Seventh, what exactly is included in the quotation and service agreement? Clarify hours, coverage, exclusions, fees and any response arrangements. Availability and response conditions should be established for the specific property; a general slogan is not a service-level commitment.

## Make the comparison useful
Give each provider the same description of the property and the support you need. Share sensitive information privately through an agreed channel. Check relevant credentials directly and ask permission before contacting references. Keep the responses together so that you can compare the proposed service consistently.

## Week 2: CCTV buying questions: coverage, storage and access

**Suggested slug:** `cctv-buying-guide`

**Meta description:** Choosing CCTV begins with the information you want the system to provide. A useful design discussion considers the property, lighting, viewing objectives,

Choosing CCTV begins with the information you want the system to provide. A useful design discussion considers the property, lighting, viewing objectives, recording requirements and who should have access.

## Define the purpose
Observing a broad area is different from obtaining detail at an entrance. Explain your priorities to the installer. The number and type of cameras should follow a site-specific assessment; a generic camera count cannot establish coverage for every property.

## Discuss real operating conditions
Lighting changes between day and night. Ask how the proposed equipment will perform in the intended setting and how it will be tested. Discuss power, connectivity and any maintenance needed. Avoid assuming that the phrase night vision guarantees useful detail in every condition.

## Understand recording and retention
Ask how footage is stored, what affects retention and who is responsible for checking that recording works. Storage needs depend on configuration and usage. Clarify how authorised footage is retrieved and how privacy is protected.

## Control remote access
Use unique credentials and appropriate account permissions. Ask your installer about supported software, updates and secure configuration. Keep access limited to authorised users and review it when staff or responsibilities change. Do not publish camera locations or screenshots that expose client information.

## Week 3: Alarms, CCTV and guards: how to discuss the right mix

**Suggested slug:** `alarms-cctv-guards`

Alarms, cameras and guards serve different roles. Deciding on a combination starts with the property and the risks you want to address, rather than with a fixed package chosen before the site is understood.

## Understand the roles
An alarm can provide an alert when a configured event occurs. CCTV can support observation and review. Guards can provide an on-site presence and carry out agreed duties. Each has limits, and none should be treated as a guarantee that an incident cannot happen.

## Connect technology to procedures
Ask what happens when an alert is received, who verifies it and how escalation is arranged. Clarify operating hours, contact details and coverage. If monitoring or response is proposed, establish the actual terms, availability and responsibilities for your location.

## Include maintenance and handover
Systems need appropriate checks and maintenance. Agree how faults are reported and how authorised users will be trained. For guarding, discuss site instructions, reporting and supervision. Keep access credentials and operational information private.

## Compare complete proposals
Look at installation, recurring charges, maintenance and exclusions alongside the initial price. A lower figure may describe a different level of support. Ask each provider to explain the assumptions behind its recommendation.

## Week 4: A practical home security checklist before travelling

Preparing a home before travel is easier when the household has a simple checklist. Focus on access, working systems, trusted contacts and the information you share.

## Check access and maintenance
Check that doors, windows and gates close and secure properly. Address known faults in locks and lighting before departure. Store ladders and tools securely. Maintain reasonable visibility around entrances without publishing details of the property's layout.

## Check systems using approved procedures
If you have an alarm or CCTV system, follow the installer or provider's instructions for testing. Confirm who receives relevant alerts and how faults should be reported. Do not trigger an emergency response as a test without the provider's agreed procedure.

## Agree trusted contact arrangements
Decide who can check the property and how they will contact you. Share access information privately and only with people who need it. Keep verified emergency and service contacts accessible to the appropriate household members.

## Review public posts
Avoid announcing detailed travel plans or posting updates that show the home is empty. Check who can see your social-media content. Delaying holiday photos until you return is a simple way to reduce unnecessary disclosure.
